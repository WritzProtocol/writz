Writz is the way to get dollars from your Bitcoin without selling it and without handing it over: the BTC stays on Bitcoin, in a script tied to your own key, and the USDC loan lives on Stellar.

## Who we serve first

| Segment | Their problem today | What Writz offers | How they check it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-custody holder in Latin America** | To get dollars, they sell their BTC or give it to a platform that holds it | Dollars without selling and without giving up custody | A full cycle on testnet: lock, borrow, repay, release |
| **Stellar lender with its own USDC** | USDC sitting idle, or supplied to Blend | The interest paid by borrowers whose collateral is native BTC | See where the interest comes from and what happens if a borrower does not repay |
| **Bitcoin institution or reserve** | BTC that cannot be sold or leave its control, and sits idle | Liquidity without a custodian, verifiable on chain | A technical evaluation and a testnet pilot |
| **Stellar integrator** | Verifying Bitcoin payments or locks from a contract | An SDK and a light client already deployed | A test integration |

<Note>
These segments are working hypotheses. So far we have a small number of recorded interviews, which give us direction, not proof. [Phase 1](/go-to-market/phases#phase-1-validation) tests them, and this page changes if the evidence disagrees. Who may use Writz at launch is a separate legal question.
</Note>

### Not yet

| Group | Why not yet |
|---|---|
| Privacy-focused users | Writz Lend positions are public on chain |
| Traders looking for leverage | A conservative, capped launch does not serve them |
| Anonymous lenders in the open | Lenders start from conversations in which they give their conditions in writing |

## The message

One sentence, for anyone who needs to hear it once:

> Borrow dollars against native BTC, without a custodian holding it and without wrapping it.

### Liquidation comes first

In our first interviews, the first reason holders gave for not borrowing was liquidation and the habit of selling and buying back, not custody. So every talk and every interview starts there, with numbers:

- You can borrow up to a 150% collateral ratio, and a position is liquidated below 120%.
- Borrowing the maximum, a 20% fall in BTC liquidates. Borrowing half of it, the position survives a 60% fall.
- On mainnet, a liquidation executes a transaction the borrower signed in advance: part of the BTC repays the debt and the remainder returns to the borrower in the same transaction. In a sharp fall there may be no remainder. [How the pre-signed steps work](/architecture/technical-overview#pre-signed-liquidation-steps)
- The trust that remains is stated as it is: two of the three protocol signers acting with a keeper could force the liquidation of a healthy position. They can never redirect the Bitcoin.

With holders, we talk about Bitcoin first ("your coins do not leave Bitcoin") and about Stellar second, as the place where the dollar loan lives. With institutions: the BTC does not leave Bitcoin, there is no custodian, and anyone can verify the position on chain.

### For the borrower

| What they ask | What we say |
|---|---|
| Why not just sell? | Selling ends your position. A loan keeps it, at the cost of interest and the risk of liquidation. If you would be glad to rebuy later at any price, selling may be the better choice |
| What happens if the price falls? | You see your ratio, you can repay at any time, and liquidation has a known threshold. We explain this before the first borrow, not after it |
| Who can touch my Bitcoin? | It stays on Bitcoin, in a script that needs your key on every path. [See the script](/how-it-works/bitcoin-side) |
| What if Writz disappears? | A timelock lets you recover the Bitcoin alone. [The procedure is public](/how-it-works/manual-emergency-recovery) |
| Is it audited? | Not yet, and there is no mainnet until it is. [Audit status](/security/audits) |

### For the lender

| What they ask | What we say |
|---|---|
| Where does the interest come from? | From borrowers. The rate is variable, and it can be zero while nothing is borrowed. We never promise a fixed return |
| What backs the loans? | Bitcoin locked in scripts on Bitcoin, verified on Stellar by a light client contract. Today a liquidation moves no Bitcoin, and the borrow cap bounds that exposure. [How liquidation works](/products/writz-lend) |
| What can go wrong? | Smart contract failure, a bad price, or a loan that is not repaid. [The security model](/security/security-model) states each one |
| Can I withdraw? | You can withdraw what is not lent out. High borrowing can make you wait |

### For institutions and reserves

| What they ask | What we say |
|---|---|
| Does the Bitcoin leave our control? | No. It stays on Bitcoin in a script that requires your key on every path, and no custodian is added |
| Can we verify it? | Positions and proofs are public on chain, so an auditor or the public can check them. An institution that needs confidentiality should weigh this |
| What is the risk? | Liquidation, smart contract failure and price feed failure, as stated in [the security model](/security/security-model) |
| What is the first step? | A technical evaluation and a testnet pilot, with no real funds |

### For projects that integrate

| What they ask | What we say |
|---|---|
| What can we build today? | Bitcoin payment verification and lending flows on testnet. [Start here](/developers/integrate) |

## What the message must not do

- **Hide liquidation.** It is the first thing a careful holder asks about, so it comes first in our explanations too.
- **Promise a return.** We describe how interest works, never how much it will be.
- **Claim more than we have.** Testnet is called testnet, a letter of intent is never called a partnership, and a conversation is never called an agreement.
- **Make the safe option look bad.** Selling or holding can be the right answer. People trust advice that sometimes says no.
