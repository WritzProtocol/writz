This page describes what we watch, not what we expect. We do not publish targets or forecasts, because a figure given before there is evidence behind it is only a hope. What we can commit to is how we measure, and how we report what we find.

## The borrower journey

Each stage is a person doing something they were not doing before.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Hears about Writz">
    Through a conversation, a community or a partner. We note where they came from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Understands it">
    They can say what Writz does and what happens if the price falls.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tries it">
    They complete a Bitcoin lock and a borrow, first on testnet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Finishes it">
    They repay the loan and their Bitcoin is released. This is the moment the promise is proven to them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Comes back">
    They take another loan, or bring someone with them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The lender journey

<Steps>
  <Step title="Hears about Writz">
    Through the Stellar community, or a product they already use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Understands the risk">
    They can say where interest comes from and what could cause a loss.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Supplies USDC">
    First test USDC, then real USDC after launch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stays">
    They keep their supply in the pool through a full cycle of borrowing, and they withdraw when they choose to.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The builder journey

<Steps>
  <Step title="Discovers the tools">
    Through the docs, an event or another builder.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Makes a first call">
    Their code calls a Writz contract or the proof API.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ships">
    They have something that real people can use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keeps it running">
    It is still in use after the event or bounty has ended.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The institution journey

<Steps>
  <Step title="First conversation">
    We understand their mandate and what they would need.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Technical evaluation">
    Their team reviews the design and the security model.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Written agreement">
    A signed document with a defined scope and next step.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pilot">
    A test on testnet, with no real funds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Live use">
    Limited use on mainnet, within the protocol's limits.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## One constraint at a time

At every point we name the single stage where most people drop out, and that stage is where the team's effort goes. If people do not finish a loan, more reach only sends more people to the same problem. This keeps a small team from working on everything at once.

## Rules for every figure we report

- **On-chain figures can be checked.** Wallets, transactions and loans come from public contract data, so anyone can verify them.
- **Testnet is labelled as testnet.** Activity with test coins shows that the system works. It is not demand for real loans.
- **Wallets are not people.** We report wallets and accounts, and we say so.
- **Agreements are stated as they are.** We say what was signed and at what stage, and we name an organization only with its consent.
- **Interviews are a signal, not a statistic.** We state how many people we spoke with, and we do not turn a few conversations into a percentage of the market.
- **Dates come with numbers.** Any figure we publish states when it was measured.
- **Interest is described, never promised.** We explain how the rate works and do not forecast returns.

## What would change this plan

This plan will change as we learn. A different first customer, a better channel or a lender design that does not work would each rewrite part of this section, and we will update it when that happens. The pages stay public, so that people can hold us to what we said and see why it changed.
